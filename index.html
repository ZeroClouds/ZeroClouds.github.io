Game Art and its Impact

Why is Game Art so Important?

Art in video games is incredible important because it helps in creating a visual for the story being told. It is supposed to be appealing to not just the eye, but to the story itself. The art in a game helps communicate a message without using words, which is why I think it is so important to have and make right when producing work.

How does Game Art Communicate?

When it comes to making art for games, the visuals in the game assist in telling the story even when there may not be a direct story being told. An example of this could be a fps game with a series of maps that are based off of ruined cities. The art in that situation would depict the story of an abandoned city that has now resulted to war. Another indicator that helps tell a story within art is color. Using certain color palettes can help evoke emotions and give a player a reading on the situation that may be upcoming depending on the colors of a character or scene. For example, red is often associated with either passion or evil, yellow is often associated with warmer feelings like joy and comfort, and blue is associated with calmer or more meloncholy feelings like sadness or relief.

My Favorite Game Art and Why

Stardew Valley

Stardew Valley a fantastic job of making the player feel comfortable and warm with its colors while also making a semi nostalgic feeling in the pixel art style. The characters all have well thought out designs and the backgrounds are all very pleasing to the eye and really immerse you into the cozy farming sim.

Until Then

Until Then combines both a 2D and 3D look seamlessly while maintaining a beautiful pixel artstyle. The characters are all animated and shown in a 2D form whilst every background is 3D with pixel UV wraps to make each scene and level feel as if you are in the room with the characters

Detroit Become Human

Detroit Become Human is a 3D game that uses motion capture to create the animations for its characters. The animations and character designs really help you attach to their personalities. Additionally, this game takes place in the future and the backgroud/level designs are so well thought out and displayed that it really makes you feel like you are in the future!

Night in the Woods

Night in the Woods uses a simplistic artstyle that allows for a more cartoon-like feel to the adventures you see when playing as Mae. It gives the game a softer feeling for the dark secrets that lie underneath the surfacae with the saturated colors of the game create a warm, energetic feeling while playing the game.

Unpacking

Unpacking has simple game mechanics but is supported heavily by the bright, cute, and nostalgic pixel art that it provides. The little details in the items you unpack from place to place as the game progresses asssist in telling a story of how the unseen protagonist you play as has grown up not just in age, but as a person. You can see throughout the game that she brings the same stuffed animal to every house she lives in along with certain cups, plates, shirts, and other items that progress forward throughout the time in-game to show how her interests have changed and grew as she has gotten older.
